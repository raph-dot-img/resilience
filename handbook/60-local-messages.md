# Local messages

Write to people nearby without the Internet. Computers on the same local
network (the same Wi-Fi or cable network) that have local messaging find
each other by themselves. This exists only where the administrator has
enabled it: then COMMUNICATIONS appears on the main menu.

WRITING TO SOMEONE
  Open COMMUNICATIONS > Local messages.
    p            people heard on the local network; Enter writes to one
    n            write to an address someone gave you
    m            your own address, to give to others
    Enter        open a conversation;  w  write a message
  A message is kept on this computer and tried again until it arrives,
  for up to 7 days. The other computer must be on, and on the same
  network, at some moment during that time.

WHAT IS SHOWN BESIDE EACH MESSAGE
    waiting      kept here, not yet arrived; it will be tried again
    sent         handed to the network; no proof that it arrived yet
    delivered    the other computer confirmed that it has the message
    FAILED       given up (cancelled, or 7 days passed); the reason shows
  Only "delivered" means it arrived. It does not mean it was read.
  Press c on a waiting message to give up on it.

WHO IS WHO
  A person is their address: 32 characters, shown in groups of four.
  A name in quotes with a question mark, like "marie"?, is only what that
  address says about itself. Anyone can announce any name. Compare the
  address with the person, face to face or by voice (i shows all of it),
  then press a to give it a name of your own.
  NOT VERIFIED beside a message from them means it could not be checked
  against that address's key (yet). Treat it with care.

GOOD TO KNOW
  Messages are encrypted for the person you write to; others on the
  network cannot read them, but they can see that your computer takes
  part. Messages stay on the local network: this is not the Internet and
  does not reach people elsewhere. Some Wi-Fi networks (hotels, public
  places) keep computers apart: nobody appears there.
  Your address and messages belong to your account on this computer and
  are included in your backups. A new account has a new address.
  The software underneath (Reticulum and LXMF) has its own licence: it
  may not be used to harm people or to train artificial intelligence.
